# Pre-training Visual Dexterity in Simulation

## Source Links

- Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15917
- Project and autonomous videos: https://spd.bot/

## TL;DR

SPD treats simulation as an on-embodiment pre-training data source for dexterous hands: operators control the target 56-DoF bimanual embodiment in VR, a causal diffusion transformer learns from 75 hours of those trajectories, and only 1–2 hours of real demonstrations are needed per downstream task. The official autonomous rollouts show strong transfer on plate racking, cup stacking, Jenga, mug hanging, and bottle tossing, while the current release lacks public code and data links.

## Novelty

Unlike human-video pipelines, SPD avoids pose estimation and retargeting; unlike physical teleoperation, it makes resets and parallel collection cheap. Its long sensorimotor history plus short action chunks also gives a concrete design for reactive contact-rich control.

## Contributions

1. A VR-to-simulator collection pipeline for on-embodiment dexterous demonstrations.
2. A 75-hour, six-scene multi-task simulation corpus collected by five operators in one week.
3. A 222M causal diffusion transformer with prefix-parallel training, sliding-window attention, and 30 Hz deployment.
4. Five-task real-robot evidence that simulation pre-training improves small-data fine-tuning.

## Task Definition

Given multi-view RGB observations, proprioception, and sensorimotor history, predict short action chunks for bimanual dexterous manipulation after simulation pre-training and limited real-task fine-tuning.

## Data / Dataset / Benchmark

- Pre-training: 75 hours of VR-teleoperated simulated demonstrations across six scenes and hundreds of objects.
- Fine-tuning: 1–2 hours of physical demonstrations per task.
- Evaluation: plate racking, cup stacking, Jenga, mug hanging, and bottle tossing on a 56-DoF bimanual system.

## Method / Pipeline

VR hand tracking drives the simulated arms and fingers through inverse kinematics. A 222M diffusion transformer consumes roughly eight seconds of image/proprioceptive history and predicts eight-step chunks. Sliding-window causal attention becomes a rolling KV cache at inference; the full policy is then fine-tuned on small real datasets.

## Evidence / Metrics / Qualitative Results

The project reports higher average progress than same-architecture behavior cloning from scratch on all five tasks. The history/chunk ablation favors a 32-step context with an 8-step chunk. Official autonomous videos show sustained bimanual contacts and recovery-worthy task sequences rather than isolated grasps.

## Limitations

SPD is not zero-shot sim-to-real: every task uses real demonstrations and full fine-tuning. It has no language conditioning, uses one custom embodiment, reports progress rather than universal success, and currently exposes no official code/data repository.

## Project Relevance

SPD is a strong recipe for generating on-embodiment contact-rich data inside executable worlds and for designing history-aware policies that remain reactive with short action chunks.
