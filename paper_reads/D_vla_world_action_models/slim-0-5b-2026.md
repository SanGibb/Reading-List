# SLIM-0.5B: Learning Action-Grounded Predictive Latents for Robot Manipulation

## Source Links
- Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09771
- Official project: https://kzzl031.github.io/slim-project-page/

## TL;DR
SLIM is a compact 0.5B latent interaction policy that learns future transitions and inverse actions through masked trajectory prediction, targeting world-model benefits without a large VLM or pixel generator.

## Novelty

A compact policy learns both forward transitions and inverse actions in predictive latent space without pixel generation.

## Contributions
- Joint forward latent prediction and inverse action reconstruction.
- Compact Mixture-of-Transformers interaction backbone.
- No extra embodied pretraining while targeting lower latency and memory.

## Task
Language-conditioned robot manipulation in simulation and the real world.

## Data
Simulation benchmarks including LIBERO-Plus plus real-robot manipulation trajectories; exact mixtures are reported in the paper experiments.

## Method
Online observation/action encoders and an EMA future encoder feed a masked trajectory predictor. The learned interaction latent conditions a flow-matching action policy.

## Evidence
Reported simulation and physical results place SLIM on a favorable parameter/performance frontier versus VLA and WAM baselines. The official first-page figure gives adequate architecture and real-world evidence.

## Limitations
- Compactness may trade away open-domain semantic capacity.
- Dataset composition and real-robot trial counts need checking before direct baseline use.
- Public code/checkpoint status was not confirmed during this run.

## Project Relevance
A useful efficient latent-world baseline and an ablation target for whether pixel prediction is necessary for action learning.

## Reproduction / Follow-up
Verify trial counts and seed variance, profile on the target robot, and compare with VLA-JEPA/JEPA-WAM under a matched backbone and dataset.
